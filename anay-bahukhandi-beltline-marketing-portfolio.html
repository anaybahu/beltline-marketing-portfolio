Campaign concept

A practical 30-day growth sprint designed for a 20-25 hour/month freelance role.

30-day sprint

Beltline Weekends, Tails Included

A content and outreach sprint that uses existing tour photos, short dog-friendly reels, and partner conversations to make the tours feel like the easiest weekend plan in Atlanta.

Week 1

Audit existing photos/video, choose 6 strong content angles, draft the first AI-assisted blog post, and build a lead list of pet businesses, hotels, apartments, and nearby restaurants.

Week 2

Publish one blog post, create two social posts, and send a first round of 15 partner emails with a clear concierge or pet-business referral angle.

Week 3

Follow up with warm leads, visit priority partners in person where useful, and create a Reel from guest questions, dog moments, and food close-ups.

Week 4

Package what worked into a repeatable monthly cadence: content calendar, outreach tracker, best-performing hooks, and next-month event ideas.

Metrics

What I would track

  • Content output: posts published, clips edited, Canva assets completed, and content reused across channels.
  • Audience response: saves, shares, comments, profile visits, link clicks, and questions that can become future posts.
  • Partner pipeline: contacts added, first messages sent, follow-ups completed, responses, and referral opportunities.
  • Tour intent: inquiries, discount-code usage, concierge mentions, and seasonal event interest.

Blog sample

A Dog-Friendly Afternoon on the Beltline: Sniffs, Snacks, and Stops

Audience: dog owners, visitors planning a weekend, local pet businesses.

Opening draft

If your dog gets excited the second the leash comes out, the Atlanta Beltline can become more than a walk. With patio energy, public art, people-watching, and built-in snack stops, it is the rare outing that feels fun for both ends of the leash. A guided Doggie Crawl turns that casual walk into a social afternoon, giving guests an easy route, photo-worthy moments, and a reason to discover local businesses they may not have found on their own.

CTA angle

Book Barks & Bites when you want a weekend plan that includes your dog, your friends, and a few new favorite Beltline stops.

Blog sample

Why Street Art Makes a Food Tour More Memorable

Audience: food tourists, Atlanta visitors, hotel concierges, date-night planners.

Opening draft

A great food tour gives guests more than a list of restaurants. On the Atlanta Beltline, the walk between bites matters too. Murals, changing public art, neighborhood history, and the rhythm of the trail make each stop feel connected to a larger Atlanta story. That mix is what guests remember later: the flavor, the color, the conversation, and the sense that they experienced the city instead of only eating in it.

CTA angle

Invite visitors to see the Beltline as a moving gallery with snacks, stories, and local flavor built into the route.
